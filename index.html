Cinema & Series

Cinema and series are more than just a weekend binge — they are a masterclass in perspective. A well-crafted film pulls you out of your own reality and drops you into someone else’s; a long-running series lets you live alongside its characters for years, watching them grow, fail and evolve. Either way, you end up understanding the quiet grief of a background character, the twisted logic of a villain, and the flawed decisions of a hero.

Music is where the two industries part ways. Hollywood keeps its score just behind the dialogue — sprawling orchestral beds or heavy synthesisers that make you feel the environment of the story rather than hear the music. Indian cinema hands music the lead role, letting a single BGM cue lift a scene into a pop-culture moment, fusing our traditional roots with modern global beats.

Then there are the lines that outlive the credits and become personal mantras — from Trivikram Srinivas and Venky Atluri to Oliver Queen, Joe West and Batman. A few of my favourites are collected on the inside pages, along with the full argument for why the score matters as much as the script.

Nature

Nature is one of the most beautiful creations the universe has ever witnessed. Long before cities, roads, and skyscrapers existed, forests stretched across the land, rivers carved their own paths, and life flourished in countless forms. What makes nature truly remarkable is not only its beauty but also its ability to heal, adapt, and regenerate.

Unlike many things created by humans, nature rarely gives up. A forest destroyed by fire can slowly return with new growth. Grass pushes through cracks in concrete. Trees take root in abandoned buildings. Rivers find new routes around obstacles. Nature constantly reminds us that life has an incredible capacity to continue, even after great destruction.

The real world gave us a glimpse of this during the COVID-19 lockdowns. As people stayed indoors for months, many noticed changes in the environment. Roads became quieter, air pollution decreased in many regions, and wildlife was seen in places where it had rarely appeared before. When people finally stepped outside after long periods of lockdown, many found cleaner skies, fresher air, and a renewed appreciation for the world around them.
