NATURE

By MEGARSHITH, Staff Writer | Filed to the Desk at Dawn

Nature is one of the most beautiful creations the universe has ever witnessed. Long before cities, roads, and skyscrapers existed, forests stretched across the land, rivers carved their own paths, and life flourished in countless forms. What makes nature truly remarkable is not only its beauty but also its ability to heal, adapt, and regenerate.

Unlike many things created by humans, nature rarely gives up. A forest destroyed by fire can slowly return with new growth. Grass pushes through cracks in concrete. Trees take root in abandoned buildings. Rivers find new routes around obstacles. Nature constantly reminds us that life has an incredible capacity to continue, even after great destruction.

This idea is portrayed powerfully in video games such as The Last of Us and Death Stranding. In The Last of Us, cities that were once crowded with people become overgrown with vines, trees, and wild plants. Nature quietly reclaims the spaces humanity left behind. The game presents a world where human civilization has fallen, yet life continues. Birds sing, animals roam freely, and plants cover abandoned streets, showing that nature does not depend on us to survive.

Similarly, Death Stranding explores the relationship between humanity and the natural world in a unique way. The landscapes are vast, lonely, and beautiful, emphasizing how small humans can be compared to the forces of nature. Mountains, rivers, rain, and open plains are not just scenery—they are challenges and reminders of the world’s power. The game suggests that rebuilding connections is important, but it also shows respect for the natural environment that exists beyond human control.

The real world gave us a glimpse of this during the COVID-19 lockdowns. As people stayed indoors for months, many noticed changes in the environment. Roads became quieter, air pollution decreased in many regions, and wildlife was seen in places where it had rarely appeared before. When people finally stepped outside after long periods of lockdown, many found cleaner skies, fresher air, and a renewed appreciation for the world around them. It was a reminder that when human activity slows down, nature begins to recover surprisingly quickly.

Nature teaches us an important lesson: regeneration is possible. Whether it is a forest recovering after a wildfire, a city being reclaimed by plants in a video game, or the environment showing signs of recovery during lockdowns, the message remains the same. Life persists. Nature adapts. Nature heals.

In a world that often moves too fast, nature stands as a symbol of patience, resilience, and hope. It reminds us that even after destruction, growth is still possible. That is why nature is not only one of the universe’s most beautiful creations—it is also one of its most enduring and inspiring.
