CINEMA

By MEGARSHITH, Staff Writer | Filed to the Desk after the last show

Cinema has had a huge impact on my life. As a fan of the Telugu Film Industry (TFI), I naturally have a deep love for movies. However, my interest is not limited to Indian cinema alone. I also enjoy Hollywood films. Although I have not explored many other film industries, Indian cinema and Hollywood are enough for me to understand and appreciate the magic of movies.

One of the biggest ways cinema has influenced me is through music. Because of movies, I started listening to music more seriously. Hollywood films mostly rely on background scores rather than songs, and over time I began to appreciate those scores. However, Indian cinema takes music to another level. Indian music directors have the ability to elevate a movie far beyond what is shown on screen. Their music can add emotion, excitement, and depth to a story in a way that feels unique.

I am not just talking about the energetic mass beats that elevate action scenes. Indian composers are equally capable of creating beautiful and emotional melodies, just like many renowned Hollywood composers. That is one of the greatest strengths of Indian film music. Composers such as Anirudh Ravichander, who worked on films like Jersey and Vikram, S. Thaman, who composed music for They Call Him OG and Ala Vaikunthapurramuloo, and M. M. Keeravani, who gave us unforgettable soundtracks in Baahubali and RRR, are just a few examples of the immense talent present in Indian cinema. There are many more gifted composers who continue to shape the industry with their music.

At the same time, I do not listen only to Indian music. I am also a fan of Hollywood film scores. Tracks such as Cornfield Chase by Hans Zimmer from Interstellar and Ludwig Göransson’s music for Oppenheimer are some of my favorites. These compositions prove that music can tell a story and create emotions even without a single word being spoken.

Another aspect of cinema that made me love movies even more is the actors and actresses. As a TFI fan, I grew up watching many Telugu heroes, but among them Nani is one of my favorites. What I like about Nani is that he does not depend only on larger-than-life action sequences or commercial formulas. Instead, he chooses unique stories and characters that feel relatable. Films like Jersey, Hi Nanna, Dasara, Eega, Shyam Singha Roy, and The Paradise show his versatility as an actor. His natural acting style makes every character believable, and that is one of the reasons why he is loved by so many movie fans.

Apart from Nani, I also admire actors such as Prabhas, Allu Arjun, Jr. NTR, Ram Charan, Mahesh Babu, Pawan Kalyan (mainly from the movie They Call Him OG), and Chiranjeevi. Their screen presence, performances, and dedication have helped Telugu cinema reach audiences across the country and around the world.

When I look beyond Telugu cinema, there are many South Indian actors whom I greatly respect. Rajinikanth is a true superstar whose style and charisma have entertained audiences for decades. Kamal Haasan is one of the most versatile actors Indian cinema has ever produced, constantly experimenting with different roles and pushing the boundaries of storytelling. I also admire Dulquer Salmaan for his charming screen presence and ability to perform in different languages and industries. Actors like Manikandan have shown that powerful performances do not always require larger-than-life characters; sometimes simple and realistic acting can leave the biggest impact on audiences.

Actresses are equally important in shaping the movies I enjoy. Sai Pallavi is one of my favorite actresses because of her natural performances, expressive acting, and incredible dancing skills. Whether it is Fidaa, Love Story, or Shyam Singha Roy, she brings authenticity to every role. I also admire Meenakshi Chaudhary, who has impressed audiences with her performances and continues to establish herself as a promising actress.

Along with them, actresses such as Samantha Ruth Prabhu, Anushka Shetty, Keerthy Suresh, and Mrunal Thakur have delivered many memorable performances that have contributed greatly to Indian cinema.

I also admire many Hollywood actors such as Leonardo DiCaprio, Robin Williams, Matt Damon, Christian Bale, Cillian Murphy, Robert Downey Jr., Tom Cruise, Keanu Reeves, Ryan Reynolds, Scarlett Johansson, Sadie Sink, and Florence Pugh. Their dedication to their roles and ability to completely transform into different characters make them some of the most respected actors in the world.

Watching actors and actresses from both Indian cinema and Hollywood helped me appreciate the effort, passion, and talent that go into creating unforgettable characters.

When it comes to directors, the topic becomes even broader. I admire many Hollywood filmmakers, including Christopher Nolan, Matt Reeves, James Gunn, Steven Spielberg, and Martin Scorsese. Their unique storytelling styles and filmmaking techniques have created some of the most memorable films in cinema history.

In Indian cinema, one director who stands out is S. S. Rajamouli. He played a major role in bringing Indian films to a global audience. Through films like Baahubali and RRR, he demonstrated what Indian cinema is capable of achieving with strong storytelling, vision, and execution. He helped popularize the concept of “Pan-Indian” cinema and inspired filmmakers across the country. Alongside him, directors such as Shankar, Lokesh Kanagaraj, Trivikram Srinivas, and many others continue to push the boundaries of Indian filmmaking and contribute to its growth.

For me, cinema is much more than a form of entertainment. It introduced me to incredible music, unforgettable stories, talented actors, inspiring directors, and different perspectives from around the world. Every movie teaches me something new, whether it is about life, emotions, creativity, or human nature. Cinema has shaped many of my interests and continues to inspire me every day.

My love for movies goes beyond simply watching them—I enjoy understanding the music, appreciating the performances, analyzing the storytelling, and experiencing the emotions they create. That is why cinema will always hold a special place in my life. Watching movies is not just a hobby for me; it is something that has become a part of who I am.
